The Master-Planned Upgrade

Trading Aging Suburban Tracts for Contemporary Innovation

As San Jose’s older housing stock in West San Jose, Cambrian, and South San Jose ages, homeowners facing six-figure renovation budgets are making the 25-minute move north up I-680 to Dublin. Here, master-planned developments like Boulevard, Dublin Ranch, and Schaefer Ranch offer move-in ready luxury, community rec centers, and state-of-the-art schools.

● Life in San Jose

  • Parcels: 1960s – 1975 single-family tract homes; 8-ft ceilings; compartmentalized rooms.
  • Infrastructure: Aging electrical panels, outdated sewer lines, continuous upkeep costs.
  • Density: High street congestion, limited new parks, variable school district boundaries.
  • Pricing: $1.7M – $2.4M for homes needing substantial modern remodeling.

● Life in Dublin

  • Parcels: Built 2010–2024; soaring 10-ft ceilings; expansive great rooms; chef kitchens.
  • Innovation: Pre-wired EV charging, whole-home solar, energy-star HVAC, smart automation.
  • Recreation: The Wave Aquatics Complex, 30-acre Emerald Glen Park, Iron Horse Trail.
  • Schools: Brand new Emerald High School and award-winning Dublin High STEM academies.
Commute Blueprint

Effortless Transit Across the South Bay Corridor

Dublin is geographically positioned at the crossroads of Interstates 580 and 680, making hybrid tech commuting remarkably smooth.

Southbound I-680 & Express Lanes

Driving south from Dublin to North San Jose, Cisco, Samsung, and Silicon Valley tech corridors takes 25 to 35 minutes. Reverse commute patterns ensure quick evening returns back to Dublin.

Dual BART Stations

With both West Dublin/Pleasanton and Dublin/Pleasanton stations within city limits, residents have unparalleled rapid rail access to downtown Oakland, San Francisco, and SFO Airport.

ACE Train Connectivity

Just minutes south at Pleasanton Station, the Altamont Corridor Express carries tech commuters in comfort to Santa Clara Great America and San Jose Diridon stations with quiet cars and onboard power.

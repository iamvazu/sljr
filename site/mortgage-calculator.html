Frequently Asked Questions: California Mortgages & Taxes

For 2024 through 2026, the FHFA conforming high-balance limit for Alameda and Contra Costa counties is $1,149,825 for a single-family home. When your loan amount exceeds this benchmark, it is classified as a Jumbo Mortgage. Jumbo loans are portfolio-held by lenders and require specialized underwriting, such as 6 to 12 months of post-closing liquid reserves, proof of asset liquidity, and verified debt-to-income (DTI) ratios typically under 43%.
Under California Proposition 13, your property tax assessment begins at 1% of the established purchase price upon transfer. On top of the base 1%, local voter-approved bonds and special assessments (such as school bonds, park districts, and Mello-Roos community facilities districts) are added. In Alameda County, the combined effective rate typically averages ~1.18%, while Contra Costa County averages ~1.15%. Proposition 13 limits future annual property assessment increases to a maximum of 2% per year regardless of broader market appreciation.
On conventional financing, Private Mortgage Insurance (PMI) is required whenever your down payment is less than 20% of the home's purchase price (loan-to-value ratio above 80%). PMI protects the lender in the event of default. Once your loan principal is paid down to 80% of the original purchase value (or if property appreciation creates sufficient equity supported by a formal appraisal), you can petition your servicer to drop PMI. Federal law mandates automatic cancellation once principal reaches 78% of original value.
Many high-net-worth buyers in Pleasanton, Danville, and Alamo choose 7/1 or 10/1 ARMs. These instruments fix your rate at an attractive discount for the initial 7 or 10 years, after which the rate adjusts annually based on benchmark SOFR rates. If you anticipate corporate liquidity events, equity vesting, or plan to refinance or relocate within 5 to 7 years, an ARM can offer substantial interest savings compared to a traditional 30-year fixed loan.
In addition to your down payment, buyers should typically budget 1% to 2% of the purchase price for escrow and closing fees. These include title insurance, county transfer taxes (customarily split or paid according to regional custom), lender origination and appraisal fees, prepaid homeowner insurance, and prorated property tax impounds for your escrow reserve account.
